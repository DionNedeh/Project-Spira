A place to begin

Digital does not mean
without impact.

Data centers power AI alongside many other digital services. They use electricity for computing and cooling; some also consume water on site, while generating electricity can use water elsewhere. The consequences depend on local grids, watersheds, technology and demand.

Spira is an early-stage public resource, not a fund or a measurement service. Voelori hopes to contribute resources in the future; no donation program or contribution is claimed here.

01 / Understand

What we know so far

The numbers below describe all data centers, not AI alone. Estimates and projections are not facility measurements or predictions of a single AI request.

01

Electricity is growing

The International Energy Agency estimates global data centers used about 415 TWh in 2024, roughly 1.5% of world electricity consumption. Its 2030 base case projects around 945 TWh. Both figures cover more than AI; the future is uncertain.

Read the IEA analysis ↗
02

Water has many paths

Water can be consumed in cooling at a facility and indirectly in electricity generation. Cooling methods, water sources, weather and local water stress all matter. Consumption and withdrawal are different measures.

03

Local context matters

A global total cannot tell a community what a particular facility will use or how its water supply and power system will be affected. Ask for local, independently verifiable information.

Explore / Follow the connections

Where does the water go?

Open each step to see how computing, electricity and water can be connected. The path varies by facility and location.

01At the data center Cooling and local water

Computers release heat. Some cooling systems use water, and some of that water can evaporate. Others rely on different cooling designs; a facility’s water source and seasonal demand matter.

02On the electricity grid Water beyond the facility

Electricity generation can also use water. This indirect footprint depends on the power sources serving the grid and how their water use is accounted for. It should be distinguished from on-site use.

03In the watershed Why place matters

The same quantity can have different consequences in different places and seasons. Consider local water availability, other users, ecosystems, water quality and drought conditions alongside the amount used.

For the underlying data-center analysis, see the Berkeley Lab report and our research notes.

A useful distinction

Withdrawal ≠ consumption

Withdrawal is water taken from a source. Consumption is the portion not returned immediately to the same source, for example through evaporation. They answer different questions and should not be treated as interchangeable.

Looking ahead

Exploring a more local path.

Voelori advocates local AI and plans to continue working on the environmental questions surrounding AI. Running suitable AI workloads on your own device can reduce reliance on major centralized AI infrastructure. This is our motivation for exploring local AI: more computation can happen close to the people using it, when the task and hardware are a good fit.

Local does not automatically mean lower impact. Devices also use electricity, and manufacturing hardware has a footprint. The comparison depends on the hardware, workload, utilization, electricity supply and whether new equipment is needed. Local inference draws power from a battery or wall outlet, and that electricity can carry emissions and indirect water use. Model training, downloads and optional cloud features may still rely on remote infrastructure. Shifting a workload does not by itself prove that total energy use, water consumption or emissions fall.

Voelori has more work planned on this subject. We want to keep learning, measure carefully where possible, and share what we find as this work develops. No measured environmental saving is claimed for that future work.

02 / Participate

Small actions, wider care

There is no universal per-prompt footprint. Focus on useful action and ask for better information.

01

Ask for transparency

When a data center is proposed near you, consult local planning and utility records. Ask about expected electricity demand, water withdrawal and consumption, water source, drought plans and public reporting.

02

Use computing intentionally

Choose tools appropriate for the task and avoid unnecessary repeated processing where practical. Actual savings vary by system; individual choices do not replace responsible infrastructure decisions.

03

Support local stewardship

Find a watershed group or community energy organization in your area. Check its work, financial information and whether its priorities match the needs of the people and places it serves.

For a local proposal

Questions worth bringing to the table

  • What water source will be used, and what are the expected annual and peak withdrawals and consumption?
  • How will use change during drought, and who receives public reports?
  • What electricity demand is expected, and how will the grid supply it?

Plans are projections. Ask how actual use will be measured and reported once a facility operates.
